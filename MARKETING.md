# Marketing plan

How affiliate gear sites like this one usually grow, and what to do first for Basecamp Outfitters. Written 2026-10-09.

Sites like this almost always grow the same way: **search traffic from buying guides and reviews, plus one or two social channels that send people to them.** Ads rarely pay for themselves, because a commission of a few percent on a $200 tent doesn't cover paid clicks.

## What usually works

### 1. Content built around buying decisions (most of your traffic)

People search right before they buy, with queries like "best backpacking tent under $300", "MEC vs REI sleeping bag", or "what to bring car camping". Successful affiliate sites target those searches with:

- **Roundups:** "Best 2-person tents for 2026", "Best headlamps for camping".
- **Head-to-head comparisons:** "Big Agnes Copper Spur vs Nemo Dragonfly".
- **Single-product reviews** of popular gear.
- **Helpful guides** that link to products: packing checklists, "how to choose a sleeping bag temperature rating", beginner guides. The trip checklists fit naturally here.

**Real, hands-on testing matters more than anything else.** Google's ranking rules for product reviews favour evidence that you actually used the gear: your own photos, measurements, and what broke or annoyed you. Sites that rewrite manufacturer specs have been losing ground for years, and AI answers in search now handle the generic questions. Your own photos from real trips are the advantage over big publishers.

### 2. One visual channel

For outdoor gear, these tend to send people to affiliate sites:

- **Pinterest:** a big source of traffic for camping and packing-list content, and pins keep sending traffic for months.
- **YouTube:** gear reviews and "what's in my pack" videos. Viewers trust them, and links in the description earn commissions. It takes the most work, but it pays off best over time.
- **Instagram/TikTok:** good for building an audience, but weak at sending clicks.

Pick one and post consistently rather than spreading yourself across all of them.

### 3. Communities (carefully)

Reddit (r/CampingGear, r/Ultralight, r/camping, r/CanadaCamping), Facebook groups and forums. The norm is to be a genuinely helpful member and link only when someone asks something your guide answers. Posting affiliate links into these groups gets you banned quickly, and most of them ban affiliate links entirely.

### 4. Email list

Offer something useful in exchange for an email address, like a printable packing checklist or seasonal gear deals. Most affiliate sites say their list earns better than any other channel, especially around big sales: Prime Day, Black Friday, and MEC's and REI's seasonal sales.

### 5. The app as a feature, not a separate business

The trip checklist app gives people a reason to come back after a single search visit. Promote it from the website ("save this checklist to your phone") instead of marketing it on its own.

## Typical timeline

- **Months 0–3:** publish 15–30 solid articles. Expect very little traffic, because Google is slow to trust new sites.
- **Months 3–9:** search traffic starts. Update the articles that are getting views, and add links from them to related pages.
- **Months 9 and on:** most of the income comes from a small number of pages that rank well. Grow more articles around those topics.

Camping is very seasonal. Searches rise from February to June and peak before summer, so publish spring content by early spring.

## For this site specifically

1. **Real content comes before applying to affiliate programs.** The products are still placeholders and guides/blog posts are still to do. REI and Amazon both review your site, and Amazon's 3-sales-in-180-days clock starts at approval.
2. **Target the Canada angle.** "Best tent in Canada", MEC comparisons and Canadian park-specific checklists are much less competitive than US searches. Few sites compare MEC and Bass Pro Canada prices well.
3. **Add a guides/blog section with good SEO basics.** The sitemap and structured data are already in place.
4. **Pick one social channel to post on regularly.** Pinterest is the lowest-effort option for checklist and guide content.
